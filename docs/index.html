Everent

Design Lead at Studio Kismet, FIEA capstone, 2025 to now

A momentum-based hoverboard game across an open world of floating sky islands, built in Unreal Engine 5.7. Coming to Steam.

7 designers led from concept through production

  • Speed-gated destruction. Walls only break when you hit them fast enough. Built in C++ on Chaos Geometry Collections: two trigger volumes measure entry speed, and an impact spawns a physics field (strain plus directional and radial velocity). Momentum becomes the key, so the same system gates secrets and rewards skill.
  • Destructibles editor tool. A Window menu panel that lists every destructible in the level, exposes its tuning and fires a test projectile with in-place reset. Designers tune breakage without entering Play mode.
  • Corrupt surfaces. A C++ physical material with world-space gravity, angle-scaled repulsion and boost suppression. In a game where you can ride any surface, it is the constraint that keeps puzzles solvable.
  • Running design. Wrote the GDD and one-page specs for movement, puzzles and progression, ran sprint planning and Jira for the design team, and tuned DualSense adaptive triggers per surface.
  • Sky creatures. Prototyped a compute-shader flocking plugin for koi, manta rays and jellyfish. When Niagara integration failed, I pivoted to instanced meshes driven by CPU readback.
  • Lighting architecture. Led the Lumen versus baked GI decision, VSM cost and HLOD plan, and wrote the team's graphics-settings spec on UGameUserSettings with runtime scalability groups.
  • Streaming-safe destruction. Hardened destructibles for World Partition and Data Layer streaming: fixed a delegate double-bind, resolved overlaps at BeginPlay and guarded destroys against unloads.
  • Playtest analytics. A local pipeline (FFmpeg, faster-whisper, DeepFace, Llama 3.1) that turns session recordings into transcripts, emotion timelines and coded feedback in the studio's spreadsheet format.

UE5.7, C++, Blueprint, Chaos, World Partition, Perforce, Jira

Spatial Engine

Personal engine, 2025 to now

A hardware ray-traced Vulkan renderer where spaces with different geometry (hyperbolic, spherical, toroidal) connect through portals, with light and shadow traced across them. Runs inside a Godot 4.6 fork and in VR.

I architect and direct it. AI writes much of the implementation; I set the goals and tests, read the GPU timings and make the call in the headset. About 145k lines of Rust across 13 crates, 600+ commits.

2.8–5.8 ms →to 0.35 ms radiance-cache resolve after streamed terrain exposed a VR bottleneck

  • Measured fixes. Streaming distant terrain pushed VR frame time up. Run data pointed at the radiance cache, so I had the distance cascade extended and checked the result pass by pass in Nsight, in the headset.
  • Lighting stack. ReSTIR direct lighting, one-bounce diffuse GI, SVGF denoising, a physically based sky and volumetric atmosphere, DLSS Ray Reconstruction, and analytic intersection shaders for curved primitives.
  • Shader regression. GPU timestamp traces showed a cylinder intersection shader eating the frame. Replaced its 64-step sphere tracer with an analytic quadratic solve.
  • Godot integration. The renderer sits behind a C ABI inside a Godot fork, so Godot supplies the editor, scripting and asset pipeline.
  • VR presence. An OpenXR path with a physics-driven avatar that grabs, climbs and handles objects, plus benchmark tooling with pinned GPU clocks and power logging.

Rust, Vulkan ray tracing, HLSL, OpenXR, Godot, Nsight Graphics

Pocket Spaces

Solo Unity prototype, 2026

Doorways that open onto rooms bigger than the building they're in. Players, physics objects and ropes all cross between them.

  • Crossing cleanly. Stencil-masked doorways, a player state machine, per-object portal membership and gravity overrides let the player and physics objects pass between differently sized rooms.
  • Room builder. One-click procedural rooms with a custom inspector: build, rebuild, eject contents and preview gravity live.
  • Rope through portals. A Verlet rope and grapple, with a renderer that follows the rope through doorways.
  • Impossible interiors. A raymarched hyperbolic Mandelbox inside one room, with Möbius-transform camera movement. I documented where stencil nesting breaks: depth 3 and beyond collapses the render queue.

Unity URP, C#, stencil rendering, editor tooling
