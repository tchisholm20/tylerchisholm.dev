Everent

Design Lead · 7 designers on a 20+ person team · UE5.7 · Oct 2025 to now

Hoverboard game across floating sky islands where you can ride any surface. Coming to Steam.

What I did

  • C++ on Chaos Geometry Collections. A sweep ahead of the board measures speed into the wall. Above the threshold the wall stops blocking a frame early, so you keep momentum, then shatters at the impact point. Used to hide secrets.

  • Editor panel listing every destructible in the level with its settings, a test shot at any point and speed, and a reset. Designers tune without entering Play.

  • Saves a safe spot every 2 seconds: grounded, slope, speed, overhead clearance, all five footprint points on static ground. Checkpoints override it and save to disk. Velocity and board state are zeroed before the teleport.

  • The one thing you can't ride. A C++ physical material that pushes the board away, scaled by approach angle, and turns off boost.

  • GDD and specs for movement, puzzles and progression. Sprint planning in Jira. DualSense adaptive-trigger tuning per surface.

  • Compute-shader flocking for the sky creatures, shipped as instanced meshes after Niagara didn't work out. The Lumen vs. baked GI decision and graphics settings. World Partition fixes for destructibles. A local pipeline that turns playtest recordings into transcripts and coded feedback.

UE5.7, C++, Blueprint, Chaos, World Partition, Perforce, Jira

Spatial Engine

Solo project · Rust, Vulkan ray tracing, OpenXR · 2026 to now

Path tracer that runs in VR inside a Godot fork. Current game: a VR climb on the real Grand Canyon.

About 115k lines of Rust and 40k of HLSL, mostly written by agents I direct (how).

What I did

  • On a Quest over Link with a laptop RTX card, across about 900 km of streamed terrain with volumetric clouds. Every feature gets a stereo frame budget before it's built.

  • The player is a Jolt active ragdoll with a contact-based support model for walking and climbing. NPCs are powered ragdolls with a wound list: stab, stick, carve, fractures, bleeding, knockouts.

  • USGS 3DEP lidar, 10 rock formations, look tuned against real photos from matched camera positions.

  • ReSTIR direct lighting, one-bounce diffuse GI, SVGF, physical sky and atmosphere, DLSS Ray Reconstruction. Analytic intersection shaders for curved shapes.

  • Portals between spaces of different curvature, with light traced through them.

  • Found with GPU timestamps: a 64-step sphere tracer, replaced with an analytic solve.

Rust, Vulkan ray tracing, HLSL, OpenXR, Godot, Nsight Graphics

Pocket Spaces

Solo prototype · Unity, C# · 2026

Doorways into rooms bigger than the building around them.

What I did

  • Stencil-masked doors into bigger rooms. The player, physics objects with per-room gravity, and a rope all cross.

  • Verlet rope that keeps working through a doorway, drawn on both sides.

  • One-click room builder with a custom inspector.

  • Raymarched hyperbolic Mandelbox with Möbius-transform movement. Stencil nesting breaks at depth 3.

Unity URP, C#, stencil rendering, editor tooling

Star Fox Adventures on PS Vita

Solo project · C, GXM · released 2026 · source on GitHub

Star Fox Adventures running natively on a PS Vita, not emulated. I wrote the graphics backend.

Built on SFA-Decomp (Zachary Canann, Jack Price-Burns and contributors) and Cassieandstuff's PC port. No game data included.

What I did

  • Translates the GameCube's GX graphics API to GXM, the Vita's native API, with no changes to the upstream port.

  • TEV combiners compiled to cached shaders. Display lists converted to vertex buffers on first draw. GPU skinning.

  • Game and render threads on separate cores. 30 fps at 640×368 in most areas.

C, GXM, VitaSDK, vitaGL, ARM

How I work with agents

I use AI agents for most of the code on my personal projects, usually a few at a time. This is the setup.

Backlog
Linear, with at most five things in progress. Every issue needs a "done when"; engine features also need a stereo frame budget.
Verified numbers
Gates that refuse to report a performance number unless the run was clean: refresh rate, power trace, sample count. Visual changes need a pre-registered threshold and a human look at the images.
Concurrency
Agents claim files and lock the GPU, headset and engine build. Every test is tied to the hash of the build it ran on.
Review
Separate reviewer agents try to break big designs before they're built.
Cost
Every session is booked to a Linear issue: 179 sessions, 571 subagents, about 570 agent-hours as of November.
Research
A local knowledge base of rendering papers (arXiv, SIGGRAPH and others) that agents search for prior art.
